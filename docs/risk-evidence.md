# Pathfinder: risk-model evidence for CT-guided PTNB

Literature grounding for the needle-path complication model. Every number below was read in the cited
source (abstract or full text) during this search. Where a source gave no number, the table says so.
Confidence: **solid** = meta-analysis, or a large adjusted cohort with a CI. **shaky** = single small
study, no CI, unclear units, high heterogeneity, or read only second-hand.

Abbreviations: PTX = pneumothorax, CT = chest tube/drain, OR = odds ratio, NS = not significant.

## 4. Suggested model coefficients

These are modelling choices built on the effects above. They are not a validated model. Apply as
`p = 1 / (1 + exp(-(b0 + Σ β·x)))`. Each β = ln(OR). The ORs come from different studies with different
adjustment sets, so stacking them double-counts somewhat. Present the output as a relative risk tier
(low/med/high) next to the cited evidence, not as a calibrated probability.

### 4a. Pneumothorax (any)

| Factor (x) | β | How to apply | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerated lung length L (cm) | +0.54 per cm | `min(L, 4)`. Cap is a modelling choice: data plateau past ~3 cm (Ozturk); Huo >5 cm OR 8.47 ≈ e^(0.54·4) | Lee 2016 (Heerink 1.05/mm ≈ +0.49/cm agrees) |
| Pleural-based, L = 0 | −0.55 | add only when the path crosses no aerated lung | Huo 2020 (1/1.73) |
| Fissure crossed | +1.32 | per path, yes/no | Huo 2020 |
| Bulla crossed | +1.81 | yes/no. Also raise a hard warning | Huo 2020 (1 study) |
| Emphysema along path | +0.74 | path-level, e.g. low-attenuation voxels on the path | Drumm 2019 |
| Emphysema (patient-level) | +1.20 | same for all paths. Use COPD +0.63 instead if no CT grading; do not stack the two | Huo 2020; Wiener 2011 |
| >1 pleural puncture | +0.89 | if the plan needs re-puncture or more than one pleural entry | Huo 2020 |
| Lesion ≤2 cm | +0.68 | patient-level | Huo 2020 |
| Position | −0.99 biopsy-side-down lateral; +1.16 lateral with biopsied lung up; 0 prone/supine | | Drumm 2019; Huo 2020 |
| Needle ≤18G | +0.44 | vs thinner needles | Huo 2020 |
| Needle–pleura angle | 0 | pooled effect is null; show a soft "<45° oblique" note citing Hiraki/Ko | Huo 2020 |
| Lower lobe, age, dwell time, coaxial | 0 | pooled NS | Huo 2020; Ko 2001 |

**Intercept:** `b0 = −1.89`. A reference path (L = 1.5 cm, every other x = 0) then gives 25.3%, the
Heerink core-biopsy pooled rate. Sanity checks: L = 0 gives 8% (raw cohorts report 0–2%, so this is
conservative); L = 3 gives 43%; L = 3 plus a fissure gives 74%; reference path biopsy-side-down gives 11%.

**Chest-tube variant (optional):** `b0 = −3.48` (3.0% reference, from Yoon's 10,568-biopsy cohort; the
pooled 5.6–6.9% includes emphysema patients). Use β = emphysema +1.86, bulla +2.40, fissure +1.26,
non-pleural-contact +0.68, needle ≤18G +0.33, COPD +0.92 (instead of emphysema). No per-cm depth OR
for chest tube was found, so leave depth out or flag it qualitatively.

## 5. Contraindications and post-procedure monitoring

The BTS guideline (Manhire et al., *Thorax* 2003;58:920–936, PMID 14586042) could not be read directly:
PMC and BMJ blocked automated access. Its content below comes from sources that cite it.

- **FEV1 <35%:** "Patients should have recent spirometry (FEV <35% needs MDT review)". This is a Royal
  College of Radiologists audit template that cites BTS 2003. Reid & Wallis, *Br J Hosp Med*
  2017;78(12), doi 10.12968/hmed.2017.78.12.c178, say the same: if FEV1 <35%, biopsy should not proceed
  without MDT discussion. It is a relative contraindication, not an absolute one.
- **Other relative contraindications** (Reid & Wallis 2017): contralateral pneumonectomy, positive-pressure
  ventilation, pulmonary arterial hypertension. INR should be <1.3.
- **Bullae:** no guideline statement on bullae in the path was read. The evidence is Huo 2020 (PTX OR 6.13,
  chest tube OR 11.04), and centres route around "obvious emphysema or bulla" (Li C 2021 methods).
  Treat it as a hard warning in the UI.
- **Audit targets** (RCR, citing BTS): PTX <20%, PTX needing drainage <3%, haemoptysis <5%, death
  <0.15%. Chest radiograph or focal CT **1 hour** post-procedure.
- **Timing evidence:** of 160 PTX, 89% were seen immediately, 9% first at 1 h and 2% first at 4 h. All
  PTX needing intervention were seen by 1 h (Perlmutt, AJR 1986, PMID 3485899). Delayed PTX (absent at
  3 h, present later) occurred in 3.3% (15/458), and 3 of those 15 needed a tube (Choi, Chest 2004, PMID
  15539721). In 3,354 biopsies, 25.1% of PTX appeared during follow-up rather than on intraprocedural
  imaging (Lim WH 2018).
- **Typical pathway** (Reid & Wallis 2017): CXR at 1 h. If a PTX is seen, repeat the CXR 1 h later and
  discharge if stable or improved. Patients with a small PTX or haemorrhage lie biopsy-side-down, avoid
  coughing, and get low-flow O2. Ambulatory drains allow early discharge.
- **Patient warning symptoms** (King's College Hospital NHS patient leaflet, 2024): go to ED or call 999
  for breathlessness worse than usual, excessive coughing, chest pain that worsens on deep breathing, or
  coughing up a lot of fresh blood. Small-volume haemoptysis is common, in about 5% of patients. Other
  advice: monitoring for up to 4 h, CXR at about 2 h, lie on the biopsy side for 1–2 h, no flying for
  6 weeks, live within 30 min of a hospital.
- **Outcomes:** patients with hemorrhage or chest-tube PTX had longer stays and more mechanical
  ventilation (P = .020) (Wiener 2011).

## 6. Gaps and caveats

- No published **mm threshold for needle-to-vessel distance**, and no OR for vessel crossing, was found.
- No pooled **per-cm** OR exists. The per-cm value is from one study (Lee 2016; unit inferred from cm
  tables). Pooled depth ORs are dichotomous and highly heterogeneous (I² 94%).
- **Needle–pleura angle:** the pooled analysis is null and single studies conflict, partly because they
  measure the angle differently (from the pleural tangent vs from the perpendicular).
- Not read directly: Hiraki 2010 and Saji 2002 full-text ORs, and the BTS 2003 full text.
